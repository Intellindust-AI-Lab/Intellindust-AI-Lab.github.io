TL;DR

Vision foundation models such as DINOv3 provide excellent dense features, but their quadratic self-attention makes high-resolution detection expensive. GTR (Gated Token Recurrence) asks a simple question: can detection-specialized foundation-model representations be transferred into a recurrent, linear-complexity backbone without giving up accuracy? The answer is yes — with the right distillation objective, a 2D-aware scan design, and a kernel that makes recurrence fast in practice.

Masked DINOv3 distillation

A frozen, detection-specialized DINOv3 teacher sees the full image; the GLA student sees masked patches. Three complementary losses — masked prediction, Gram matching, and a new Visible Representation Alignment — transfer the teacher's feature geometry in one stage.

Recurrence made 2D

Alternating left↔right and top↕bottom scan directions remove the directional bias of 1D recurrence, and a lightweight C2f pyramid feeds a query-based DETR decoder — giving a global receptive field from the very first block.

Hardware-aware kernel

A chunkwise CUDA operator parallelizes per-chunk contractions and keeps only a light scan over chunk boundaries: 2.6× faster than FLA at 256 tokens, 4.0× at the 1,601-token deployment shape, 6.4× at 16,384 tokens.

High-resolution scalability and a superior accuracy–efficiency trade-off. On an RTX 4090, GTR-S scales to far higher input resolutions than RF-DETR-S and EdgeCrafter-S — 5.6× less memory and an 81× throughput advantage at 16,384-pixel inputs — while establishing a better box-AP-vs-latency Pareto frontier on COCO (up to 1.5× speedup at comparable accuracy).

How GTR Works

GTR is a DETR-like detector with three components: a 12-layer gated-linear-attention (GLA) backbone that replaces quadratic self-attention with a data-dependent recurrent state, a lightweight three-scale projector that fuses features tapped from blocks 4/8/12 into a stride-8/16/32 pyramid, and a query-based detection decoder with grouped-query training. Consecutive blocks scan the patch grid left→right, right→left, top→bottom, and bottom→top, so information propagates globally in all four directions without any window partitioning.

Overview of GTR. A 12-layer GLA backbone alternates two-dimensional scan directions (top right) and exposes intermediate features to a lightweight three-scale projector; the resulting pyramid is decoded by a query-based detection head.

One-Stage Masked Distillation

Before distillation, a generic DINOv3 is first specialized for detection following the EdgeCrafter (ECDet) paradigm, then frozen. During distillation the teacher processes the complete image while the GLA student receives masked patches (mask ratio 0.5). The objective combines three complementary signals:

 Masked token prediction

$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mask}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_m|}\sum_{i\in\Omega_m}\big\|\phi(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{stu}}^{i})-\mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{tea}}^{i}\big\|_2^2$

Recover the teacher's representation at hidden positions from visible context — the classic masked-distillation signal.

 Visible Representation Alignment

$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vra}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_v|}\sum_{i\in\Omega_v}\big\|\phi(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{stu}}^{i})-\mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{tea}}^{i}\big\|_2^2$

New in GTR. An order-dependent recurrent student drifts on visible tokens even when masked prediction converges — VRA pins the features the detector actually consumes.

 Intermediate Gram matching

$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gram}}=\frac{1}{K|\Omega_v|}\sum_{k,\,i}\Big[1-\big\langle\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{k}_{\mathrm{tea}}(i,:),\overline{\mathbf{A}}^{k}_{\mathrm{stu}}(i,:)\big\rangle\Big]$

Match pairwise cosine-affinity structure at blocks 4/8/12 — transferring the token-to-token relations that dense prediction depends on.

Masked DINOv3 representation distillation. The frozen detection-specialized teacher processes the complete image; the trainable GTR student receives masked patches. Intermediate visible-token relations and final representations at both masked and visible positions provide the distillation targets.

Why VRA matters. Without VRA, a ViT student drifts only mildly on visible tokens, but the GLA student drifts substantially and persistently — its recurrent token mixing gives no free alignment. Adding VRA suppresses the drift, with a markedly larger effect on the GLA student. Ablations agree: masked prediction alone reaches 49.0 AP; adding VRA and Gram supervision lifts it to 51.9 AP (+2.9).

Global Receptive Field from Block One

Four-direction recurrence changes what the network sees. At Stage 1 (block 3), GTR-S already has global effective-receptive-field support: every location of a 1024×1024 input contributes, and the smallest square containing 99% of the gradient mass spans 1,017 px. RF-DETR-S, whose first three blocks use windowed attention, is confined to a single 512×512 window — exactly 25% of the image — until global attention begins at block 4.

Stage-wise effective receptive fields (hover over the figure to magnify). Columns show blocks 3/6/9/12 plus a zoom of the central 128×128 region. The horizontal and vertical structures in GTR's maps reflect its two scan axes; RF-DETR's Stage-1 support is limited to one attention window.

A Kernel That Makes Recurrence Fast

Chunkwise GLA evaluates the exact recurrence $\mathbf{S}_t=\mathrm{Diag}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)\,\mathbf{S}_{t-1}+\boldsymbol{k}_t^\top\boldsymbol{v}_t,\; \boldsymbol{o}_t=\mathrm{scale}\,\boldsymbol{q}_t\mathbf{S}_t$ by splitting the sequence into chunks of 64 tokens. The official FLA schedule performs an expensive matrix contraction while traversing chunks serially; GTR's inference-only operator instead computes all per-chunk summaries in parallel across the chunk–head grid and keeps only a lightweight, coalesced scan for boundary states. Fixed-shape FP16 kernels ($C{=}64$, $d_k{=}32$, $d_v{=}64$) fuse the cumulative sums, run the heavy products on tensor cores, and hold the causal tile in shared memory — exposing full GPU parallelism even at batch size one.

What Actually Matters

Every Loss Earns Its Place

Distilling GTR-S and training directly on COCO (no Objects365): masked prediction alone leaves 2.9 AP on the table. VRA and Gram supervision are complementary — one constrains where the student looks, the other how its tokens relate.
